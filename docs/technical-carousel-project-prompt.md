# Reusable project prompt

Paste the following into the chat for the project repository.

```text
Create a LinkedIn carousel and a full caption about the automation built in this repository.

Make it a problem-first build story that property-management decision makers and buyers can understand. It should also show a hiring reader how I think, solve problems and improve a build. Keep the language accessible. Do not turn it into a technical tutorial or a sales pitch.

Read the repository instructions, current progress notes, implementation and recorded checks before writing. Use current behavior rather than an outdated plan.

Use these renewal-retention references for the visual style and level of detail:
D:\codex_projects\automation_projects\renewal-retention\carousel-kit\technical-retrospective-renewal-retention.html
D:\codex_projects\automation_projects\renewal-retention\carousel-kit\out\technical-retrospective-renewal-retention.pdf
D:\codex_projects\automation_projects\renewal-retention\docs\build-4-content-strategy\technical-retrospective-carousel.md

Also inspect the annotation treatments in:
D:\codex_projects\automation_projects\maintenance-triage\carousel-kit

Inspect the rendered references as well as their source. Work in the current repository.

CONTENT

Tell a connected story: the specific problem I tried to solve, what I built, how it works in ordinary language, the important decisions, one or two real things that broke, what I changed, what I checked, and what I'd improve next.

Explain decisions through their consequences for the people using the workflow. For each bug, make clear what someone could experience, why it happened in simple terms, and what the fix changes. Let this show problem-solving ability without requiring technical knowledge.

Show where AI helps and where a person makes the decision, if relevant to this build. Do not assume the same approval rules or workflow as the reference.

Do not display a tech-stack list, node counts, schemas, code, internal status names or infrastructure diagrams. A technical detail earns space only if it is necessary to understand a meaningful decision, and it should be explained in plain language. Do not replace concrete substance with generic claims about AI or efficiency.

Choose this project's strongest material. Do not copy the reference's bugs, features or conclusions. Avoid padding to hit a slide count; roughly 8-11 slides is a guide. Give each slide one clear job. Start with the problem and what the build helps someone do, rather than its implementation.

PUBLIC WORDING

Talk about what I built. Do not mention demo, proof-of-concept, synthetic, fictional or test-data labels. Do not add disclaimers about having no clients, no deployment or no measured business results. Keep provenance in internal working notes.

Keep every public claim grounded in implementation and evidence. Do not invent deployments, customer outcomes, accuracy scores, features or safeguards. Describe recorded checks narrowly. Phrase future improvements as future work. Use first person and plain US English; avoid hype, em dashes, exclamation points, a sales pitch or a hiring plea.

DESIGN

Preserve cream ruled paper, subtle grain, Switzer, restrained Zodiak emphasis and blue/yellow annotations. Every annotation shape can be either color, including highlights, selection boxes with handles, circles, underlines and strikes. Interior selection boxes are allowed; this overrides older fixed-color and cover-only restrictions.

Make the marks meaningful: emphasize the concern, the decision, the gap or the fix. Avoid a circled word in every headline. Use varied, phone-readable layouts: simple process lanes, responsibility comparisons, before/after flows, short check/result tables and numbered lessons. Avoid dense technical diagrams and repeated paragraph-plus-tiny-flow layouts. Do not repeat the same point in the body, diagram and footer. Usually keep supporting prose to 15-30 words per slide.

Use explanatory diagrams rather than fabricated screenshots or output evidence. Keep text and annotations legible on light and dark pages.

CAPTION AND DELIVERY

Write a complete caption with context, the problem, the build, lessons from real failures and next improvements. It should read naturally as a post itself. Do not aggressively shorten it or add a stack list. Apply the same factual and public-wording rules as the slides.

Create the actual carousel PDF, editable source, slide copy, caption and contact-sheet preview. Use the repository's existing carousel tools where practical. Render and inspect every final page and the contact sheet. Check factual claims, fonts, text preservation, clipping, overlap, diagrams and privacy. Keep credentials, real contacts, internal notes and local paths outside the public content.

Open the finished PDF and share its preview for review. Do not publish it or add it to the posting queue. Follow repository documentation, commit and push requirements. Finish a rendered first version without stopping for outline approval; I will request changes after seeing it.
```
